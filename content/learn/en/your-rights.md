## The 2-hour rule

India's IT Rules 2021 were amended in February 2026 (in force from 20 February). Under **Rule 3(2)(b)**, when you complain that content shows you nude, in a sexual act, or is an artificially morphed image of you, the platform must **remove it or block access within 2 hours**. Before this, the limit was 24 hours.

The clock starts when **you complain**, not when the content was posted. Reclaim's case tracker starts a 2-hour countdown when you log a report.

## Other timelines

- Court or government takedown orders: within **3 hours**.
- Grievances about content concerning an individual: resolved within **36 hours**.
- AI-made content must carry a clear, prominent label.

## The Grievance Officer

Every large platform must have a **Grievance Officer** for India. If your report is ignored or rejected, you can write to them. The government publishes a list of these officers. Reclaim's platform guides show each route, and the drafter writes the escalation for you.

If the Grievance Officer's decision does not help, you can appeal to the government's **Grievance Appellate Committee** at gac.gov.in.

## Criminal law

| Law | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| IT Act s. 66E | Capturing or sharing images of private areas without consent |
| IT Act s. 67 and 67A | Publishing obscene or sexually explicit material online |
| BNS s. 77 | Voyeurism, including sharing images agreed to be taken but not shared |
| BNS s. 78 | Stalking, including monitoring a woman online |
| BNS s. 351 and 308 | Threats and extortion |

## What these rights don't promise

The law sets duties. It does not guarantee every platform will follow them on time. That is why Reclaim tracks deadlines and scores platforms publicly. Reclaim explains the law but is not legal advice. For your specific case, talk to a lawyer or a One Stop Centre.

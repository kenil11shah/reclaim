## What is a deepfake?

A **deepfake** is a picture, video or audio clip that a computer program has created or changed so that it looks or sounds real. The word comes from "deep learning", a type of AI.

A **morphed** image is an older idea: someone cuts and pastes your face onto another body using editing apps. AI tools now do this in seconds and make it look far more real.

## How common are sexual deepfakes?

Very common. A 2019 study by Deeptrace found that **96%** of deepfake videos online were pornographic. Later estimates put it even higher, with women as the overwhelming target.

"Nudify" apps and bots let anyone upload a normal photo and get a fake nude back. A WIRED investigation found at least 50 such bots on Telegram with about 4 million users a month combined. In January 2026, Grok, the AI tool built into X, was used to "undress" real women's photos until the Indian government stepped in.

## "But it isn't really me"

People sometimes say a fake is "not real", so it should not matter. It does matter. Others may believe it, it can be used to shame or blackmail you, and it takes away control of your own body and image. The harm is real even when the picture is not.

## What does the law say?

- Platforms must remove **artificially morphed** intimate images within 2 hours of your complaint (IT Rules, Rule 3(2)(b), as amended in February 2026).
- AI-made content must now carry a clear label.
- Publishing sexually explicit material, real or fake, can be an offence under IT Act section 67A.
- Spreading a fake with false claims about you can also be defamation.

## Can I prove it's fake?

You do not need to prove it is fake to report it. Platforms like X, Reddit, Google and Meta say their rules cover AI-made intimate images. Report it the same way you would report a real image.

## What is sextortion?

**Sextortion** is blackmail using intimate images. Someone says they have a nude or sexual image or video of you, real or fake, and threatens to send it to your family, friends or followers unless you pay money, send more images, or do something else.

It often starts with a friendly stranger on Instagram, a dating app or a video call. Sometimes the images are fake, made with AI from your normal photos.

## What to do right now

1. **Do not pay.** Paying almost never ends it. It usually leads to bigger demands.
2. **Do not delete the chat.** It is your evidence.
3. **Save evidence.** Screenshot the messages, their profile, their username, and any payment details or UPI IDs they send. Write down the date and time.
4. **Block and report** the account on the platform, after you have saved everything.
5. **Report the crime** on cybercrime.gov.in or call **1930**.
6. **Tell someone you trust.** You do not have to handle this alone.

## Will they really share it?

Some do, many do not. Blackmailers count on panic. Once you stop replying and report them, many move on to someone else. If they do share it, you can report it, and platforms must remove it within 2 hours of your complaint.

## What does the law say?

- Threatening to harm your reputation to make you act is **criminal intimidation** (BNS section 351).
- Demanding money by putting you in fear is **extortion** (BNS section 308), even if you never paid.
- Sharing the images is a further offence under the IT Act.

## If you are under 18

Please read the guide for under-18s and use the Child Helpline, 1098. Do not send any more images, and do not save the ones you have.

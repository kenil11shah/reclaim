Almost every big platform now says it bans non-consensual intimate imagery, including AI-made images. So why does it keep spreading?

## Rules are not the same as enforcement

- **Reporting is different everywhere.** Each platform has its own forms, words and steps. Some have a dedicated option for intimate images; others only have a general report button.
- **Encrypted apps can't scan.** WhatsApp and Telegram chats are encrypted, so hash-matching tools like StopNCII cannot work there.
- **No one publishes removal times.** India now has a 2-hour legal deadline, but platforms do not publish how often they meet it.

## What 2026 showed

- In **January 2026**, X's AI tool Grok was used to "undress" photos of real women. India's IT ministry issued a notice, and the UK regulator Ofcom opened a formal investigation.
- In **July 2026**, the Tech Transparency Project found more than 7,600 ads for nudify apps on Facebook and Instagram.
- The Institute for Strategic Dialogue found YouTube, X, Reddit and Facebook were the main sources of traffic to nudify tools between December 2025 and March 2026.
- In **June 2026**, an Indian government report flagged Telegram channels reported for harassment and child abuse content.

## Why a scorecard?

There was no single public place showing which platforms make reporting easy, which work with StopNCII, which have a reachable Grievance Officer, and which keep getting caught. Reclaim's **Platform Accountability Tracker** scores 8 platforms on 8 criteria. Every point links to its source and the date it was checked, and anything not checked says "Not assessed".

It is a prototype, scored by one author from public information. It is a starting point for holding platforms to the law, not the final word.

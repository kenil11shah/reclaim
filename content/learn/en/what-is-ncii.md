**NCII** stands for **non-consensual intimate imagery**. It means a nude, sexual or intimate photo or video of someone that is shared, or threatened to be shared, without their consent.

## What counts as NCII?

- A private photo or video you shared with one person, which they then passed on.
- A video recorded secretly, for example in a changing room, hostel or hotel.
- A **morphed** image, where your face is put on someone else's body.
- An **AI-made** or "deepfake" image or video that shows you nude or in a sexual act, even though it never happened.
- Threats to share any of the above, often to get money or more images. This is called **sextortion**.

Some people call this "revenge porn". Experts avoid that term, because it suggests the victim did something to deserve "revenge", and because it is abuse, not porn.

## Who does this happen to?

Anyone. You do not need to have taken an intimate photo for this to happen to you. Today, one clear photo from Instagram or WhatsApp is enough for someone to make a fake. Research on one large deepfake site found hundreds of targets who were not celebrities at all.

## Is it illegal in India?

Yes. Sharing intimate images without consent can be an offence under the IT Act (sections 66E, 67 and 67A) and the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (for example section 77 on voyeurism). Threats and money demands are separate crimes.

Since February 2026, the IT Rules say a platform must remove intimate or morphed images of you **within 2 hours** of your complaint.

## What should I do first?

1. Take a breath. It is not your fault.
2. Save the links and usernames, without forwarding the content.
3. Report it on the platform, then on cybercrime.gov.in or by calling 1930.
